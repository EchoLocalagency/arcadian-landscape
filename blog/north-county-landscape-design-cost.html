What Landscape Design Costs in North County San Diego

Published 2026-08-03 by Arcadian Landscape

What a North County landscape design package covers

Landscape design in North County San Diego generally runs $2,500 to $8,000 for a full residential plan. The package covers site measurement, a grading and drainage plan, planting selections, irrigation zoning, lighting layout, and construction details a licensed contractor can price and build from.

Last updated: August 2026

Every design starts with a walk. Soil, sun, slope, existing drains, and where the water actually goes during a February storm.

North County splits into two rough camps, and the split changes almost every decision downstream. Coastal ground in Encinitas, Cardiff, and Leucadia is sandy. It drains fast, so standing water is rarely the problem and salt wind is. Push inland past Olivenhain toward Rancho Santa Fe and Bressi Ranch and we hit heavy clay that can hold water in a 12 inch percolation hole for hours.

That one difference moves the plant palette, the irrigation run times, and the base depth under anything we pave.

How much does landscape design cost in North County San Diego?

Design is priced off lot size and how much the plan has to solve. A flat quarter acre backyard with working drainage is a different exercise than a sloped canyon lot that needs walls before it needs plants.

Construction is quoted separately off the finished plan. That order matters. A builder pricing from a real plan is pricing the same thing you are comparing against.

Why do so many North County plans stall at HOA review?

Design review committees in Santaluz, Del Sur, and Bressi Ranch look at front yard plant palettes, wall heights, fence materials, and lighting spill onto neighboring lots. We have watched otherwise finished plans sit for weeks because a proposed pilaster ran taller than the community standard.

So we pull the guidelines before drawing rather than after. It is faster and it costs nothing.

The technical parts that never show up in photos

Irrigation is where most inherited yards fail. Static street pressure around here usually lands between 45 and 75 PSI. Drip needs regulating down to roughly 30 PSI, and when that regulator is missing you get emitters popping off the line all summer. We check pressure before designing zones, not after.

Hardscape has its own rules. Patios and walkways go on 4 inches of compacted Class II base. Anything a vehicle crosses gets 6 inches, and on those inland clay lots we sometimes go deeper. Bedding sand is screeded at 1 inch, because a thick sand layer is exactly what produces the wavy settled patio you see on cheap installs three years later. More on that on our paver installation page.

Lighting transformers get sized with headroom left on the circuit. Coastal jobs get brass or stainless fixtures, since plated housings near the water corrode and start failing within a couple of seasons.

What we look at before drawing anything

  • Percolation, tested in the actual planting areas rather than assumed from the neighborhood
  • Where roof and hardscape runoff currently exits the property
  • Existing irrigation pressure, valve locations, and controller type
  • Sun and wind exposure through the day, including the June overcast pattern that fools people into overplanting shade-lovers
  • HOA guidelines and any wall over 3 feet that will need engineering and a permit

Design only, or design and build?

Some homeowners want a plan they can shop. That is fine and we sell it that way. But the drawing and the build are one conversation on our jobs, and problems found during construction get solved by the person who drew the plan.
